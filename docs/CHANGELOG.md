A microburst waits on short final, the kind that brought down Delta 191 at DFW in 1985: the airspeed jumps, then the headwind dies, a downdraft pushes you down and WINDSHEAR sounds. Full power and 15° nose up, hold it and don't dive for the lost speed, and you climb out; the card grades how fast you reacted, how well you held the nose up and how much height you lost. At Kid level the plane flies the escape for you
2026-10-30 · #0116 · The 737's engines now lose thrust with height to match CFM's published figure for its CFM56-7B at 35,000 ft and Mach 0.8: 26.5 kN per engine at full power, down from 35. At mid weight and Mach 0.79 the jet now tops out near 44,000 ft instead of 52,000. That is still a little above the 41,000 ft it is certified to fly, because its wing doesn't yet feel the extra drag near the speed of sound (ticket 0120). Down low you won't notice: takeoffs and go-arounds are about the same (under 1% less thrust at DFW, about 2% on a hot day)
2026-10-30 · #0118 · The F-16's flight tests now check a full pull: at 450 kt (calibrated) and 15,000 ft it pulls hard (at least 7.5 g), and that check passes. A known issue they found: the F-16 has no fly-by-wire limiter yet. In Pilot, hauling the stick all the way back pulls about 13 g, well past the real jet's 9 g limit, and the nose keeps coming up past the stall; flown slow, the wing goes far past the real jet's 25° angle-of-attack limit. Kid and Learner already stop the nose short of the stall, so they aren't affected. Ticket 0121 will add the limiter
